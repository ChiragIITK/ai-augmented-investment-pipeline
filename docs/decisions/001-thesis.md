# Decision 001 — Investment Thesis

## Thesis

AI-native companies building autonomous software agents that execute
high-value business workflows end-to-end.

## Why this thesis

The goal is to identify startups where AI is not merely a user interface,
copilot, or content-generation layer, but is responsible for executing
meaningful business work.

This gives us a concrete way to distinguish companies with potentially
large productivity and economic impact from generic AI applications.

## What we consider a strong fit

A company should generally satisfy most of the following:

- AI is central to the product rather than an incremental feature.
- The product executes a meaningful business workflow.
- The workflow can be completed with limited human intervention.
- The workflow has measurable economic value through time, cost, revenue,
  or operational efficiency.
- There is a plausible reason the company can become a standalone business
  rather than simply being absorbed into a foundation model or existing SaaS
  product.

## What we consider a weak fit

Examples include:

- Generic chatbots
- AI writing/content-generation tools without workflow ownership
- Thin wrappers around foundation models
- Copilots where humans still perform most of the workflow
- AI features that are incidental to the core product

## Scope

The thesis is intentionally industry-agnostic. We will consider sales,
customer support, finance, engineering, legal, operations, healthcare,
and other business workflows.

The key criterion is the nature and economic value of the workflow, not
the industry itself.
