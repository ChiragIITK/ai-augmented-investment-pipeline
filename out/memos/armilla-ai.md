## Product

Armilla AI sells liability insurance for generative AI and AI agents (One-liner). It operates as an MGA and Lloyd's Coverholder dedicated to insuring AI-related risks, positioning itself as the first YC-backed entity of this kind (YC long description). Policies cover both developers and deployers of generative AI and AI agents for performance and liability — spanning errors, hallucinations, incorrect decisions, data leakage and regulatory violations (YC long description). In plain terms, this is an insurance/risk-transfer business for AI systems, not itself an autonomous agent product.

## Risks & open questions

- Thesis mismatch: the thesis targets AI-native autonomous agents that own high-value business workflows, but Armilla is an insurance provider for AI risk, not an agent that autonomously executes a workflow (One-liner; YC long description). The rule-based score reflects this with 0/30 on workflow ownership and 0/15 on autonomy.
- Regulatory and underwriting dependency: as an MGA and Lloyd's Coverholder, the business depends on capacity providers and regulatory standing that are not detailed in the sources (YC long description) — an open question on durability.
- Single named founder in a specialized insurance domain: only Karthik Ramakrishnan is listed, and his background is AI GTM/strategy rather than insurance underwriting (Founder bio: Karthik Ramakrishnan); the broader team's insurance expertise is unknown.
- No momentum or traction signal in the sources — no hiring flag, customer, or revenue data (Score breakdown: momentum 0/10), leaving demand unproven.

## What would change our mind

- Concrete traction: bound policies, premium volume, named enterprise customers, or capacity commitments (none present in sources).
- A clearer picture of the full founding/executive team, especially insurance underwriting depth, given only one non-CEO founder is listed.
