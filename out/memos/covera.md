## Product

Covera builds ready-to-use AI agents for insurance brokers that take over day-to-day servicing work: submission intake, carrier quoting, quote comparison, claims handling, past-due collections, and account rounding. The pitch is that brokers spend time advising and selling instead of doing data entry. They target agencies of every size and connect natively to the tools brokers already use.

## Market

The target market is insurance brokers/agencies (Fintech, Insurance, AIOps tags), and the product serves agencies of every size. Why-now is not explicitly stated beyond the general premise of AI agents replacing manual servicing work.

## Risks & open questions

- Deep, regulated workflows (claims handling, carrier quoting, collections) in insurance carry compliance and error-cost risk; the sources give no evidence of how Covera handles accuracy, liability, or carrier integrations at scale.
- No traction, revenue, or customer evidence is provided — it is unknown whether any agencies are live or paying.
- Effectively a solo founder (2-person team) with no described technical/engineering co-founder, raising execution risk on a broad, integration-heavy product spanning six workflow areas.
- Native connection to 'the tools they already use' implies dependence on carrier and agency-management-system integrations that are unproven in the sources.

## What would change our mind

- Evidence of agents genuinely owning workflows end-to-end and operating autonomously (the scoring found no explicit workflow-ownership or autonomy language) — e.g. metrics on tasks completed without human intervention.
- Early traction signals: paying agencies, retention, or volume of submissions/quotes/claims processed.
- A named technical co-founder or engineering depth to de-risk the integration-heavy, regulated build.
