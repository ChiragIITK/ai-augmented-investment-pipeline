# Bluebook — 🔴 Pass

**AI for accounting**

https://getbluebook.com/ · YC Winter 2025 · Stockholm, Stockholm County, Sweden · team 4

**Score: 38/100** (weak) · _Thesis: AI-native autonomous agents owning high-value business workflows_

## Recommendation: Pass

Pass, consistent with the 38/100 (weak) score. Bluebook is clearly AI-native and targets a real accounting-firm workflow, but the sources show no explicit autonomy — the product reads more as research acceleration than an agent owning high-value workflows end-to-end — which is the core of our thesis (Score breakdown: Autonomy 0/15; Workflow ownership 8/30). Combined with no momentum signals, an aging launch, and an unquantified customer claim, there is not enough evidence to support conviction at this stage.

## Team

Two named founders (team size 4). Philip Andersson (CEO) is a self-taught AI builder and repeat founder — built a software consultancy generating millions in revenue, exited a product company, and served as an engineering leader at startups including FirstVet, Mindler and Epidemic Sound (Founder bio: Philip Andersson). Filip Stål (CTO) has a thin public bio describing himself as someone who loves to build, exercise and cook, with no stated accounting or prior-founder background disclosed (Founder bio: Filip Stål). Net: credible technical/founder depth on the CEO side; CTO relevance is an open question given the sparse bio.

## Product

Bluebook builds AI agents for accounting firms to accelerate research, reconciliations and monthly closings, with the pitch that professionals can serve 200 clients instead of 20 and cut research time by 30% (YC long description). In plain terms, it is AI software ("AI for accounting") targeting the internal workflows of accounting firms rather than end-consumers (YC one-liner; tags: B2B, Finance and Accounting, Fintech, AI).

## Market

The company frames a strong why-now: a CPA retirement wave (cited as 75% over the coming 15 years), rising complexity from globalization and new regulations, and PE consolidation driving productivity demands, with LLMs enabling context-aware software beyond rule-based tools (YC long description). It claims the 30 leading firms in the Nordics already use Bluebook (YC long description). No explicit market size (TAM/revenue), pricing, or named competitors are provided in the sources — these are unknown and should not be assumed. Geographic focus appears Nordic/Stockholm-based (Location: Stockholm, Sweden).

## Risks & open questions

- Autonomy is unproven: scoring found no explicit autonomy language, so it is unclear whether Bluebook truly owns workflows end-to-end or is an assistive tool requiring accountant sign-off (Score breakdown: Autonomy 0/15).
- The '30 leading Nordic firms already use Bluebook' claim is unquantified — usage could be pilots or trials rather than paying, retained customers; no revenue or retention data is in the sources (YC long description).
- CTO domain fit and depth are unclear given a one-line, non-specific bio, raising execution risk on a technically demanding accounting product (Founder bio: Filip Stål).
- No momentum signals (hiring, growth metrics) are available, and the launch is now over 15 months old, so traction trajectory is unverified (Score breakdown: Momentum 0/10, Freshness 4/15).

## What would change our mind

- Evidence of true autonomous workflow ownership — e.g. closings or reconciliations completed agentically with minimal human intervention — rather than research acceleration (addresses Autonomy 0/15).
- Hard traction proof: paying-customer counts, revenue, and retention behind the '30 Nordic firms' claim (YC long description).
- Clarity on CTO/team technical depth in accounting-grade automation and any post-launch momentum signals (Founder bio: Filip Stål; Score breakdown: Momentum).
