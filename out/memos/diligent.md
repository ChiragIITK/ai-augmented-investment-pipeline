## Product

Diligent builds LLM-based AI assistants for fintech risk and AML compliance teams to automate routine customer due diligence tasks (YC long description). Concretely, customers automate workflows such as running due diligence on legal entities, reviewing customer documents like registry extracts and proofs of address, and remediating false-positive alerts from AML systems (YC long description). The stated value is lowering cost-to-comply, improving risk detection, and freeing human capacity for investigations requiring expertise (YC long description).

## Risks & open questions

- Product framing is mixed between execution and assistance — described as 'AI assistants' that 'automate and strengthen' tasks while explicitly leaving investigations requiring human expertise to people (YC long description). Whether the agents truly own workflows autonomously vs. assist a human is unclear.
- No autonomy language appears in the sources, so the degree of hands-off operation is unverified (Score breakdown: Autonomy 0/15).
- Compliance/AML is a highly regulated, accuracy-critical domain; the sources provide no evidence of accuracy benchmarks, customer traction, or regulatory acceptance.
- Team is small (10 people) and the sources give no revenue, customer names, or growth metrics to validate demand (YC listing: team size 10).

## What would change our mind

- Evidence that the agents autonomously own end-to-end compliance workflows (e.g., closing alerts or completing due diligence without human sign-off) rather than merely assisting.
- Concrete traction signals — named fintech/bank customers, revenue, or measurable reduction in cost-to-comply or false positives.
- Data on market size and competitive positioning within regtech/AML that is currently absent from the sources.
